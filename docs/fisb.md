# FIS-B weather in skycue

FIS-B (Flight Information Service - Broadcast) is the weather and
aeronautical information that FAA ground stations broadcast on 978 MHz as
UAT ground uplinks. dump978-fa already hands skycued every uplink frame;
with `fisb.enable = true` (the default) skycued decodes them into products,
keeps the current picture with its expiry rules, records it, serves it over
the web API and draws it on the web map. This guide is for the operator
(what you will and will not see) and for the developer (where the rules
live). The bit-level layouts and their sources are in
[fisb-reference.md](fisb-reference.md); the API contract is in
[web-api.md](web-api.md#fis-b).

**Limits.** FIS-B in skycue is for situational awareness and curiosity.
It is not flight-critical, not certified or approved for any operational
use, and makes no claim of conformance to DO-267/DO-358 or any FAA standard.
It decodes what two open decoders agree on and what the test captures
confirm; anything else is skipped (see [What is skipped](#what-is-skipped)).
Products can be missing, late or wrong. Never use it for a flight decision.

## Turning it on and off

| Key (`[fisb]` in skycue.toml) | Default | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fisb.enable` | `true` | decode uplinks (live, from dump978-fa's raw port, or the `uat_uplink` records of a replayed recording). `false`: uplinks are still recorded in the received stream, but nothing is decoded; `/api/fisb/status` says `"enabled": false` |
| `fisb.experimental` | `false` | `true`: also decode the layouts marked **[U]** below (APDU time options 1 and 3, raster blocks at or above 60° N). For testing only |

Both are read at start; change them and restart skycued.

## What is decoded

The status column says what backs each product: **sample** means it is
decoded from a real or test capture in `tests/data/` and pinned
by a test; **vectors** means hand-built test vectors only. The test data are
the 2015 dump978 sample (`raw/uat-sample.txt.gz`, 704 uplinks from one
station near San Jose; its counts first) and `fisb/tg30.978`, a test group
generated by the fisb-decode project with 2020 timestamps (963 uplinks;
second count). Counts are decoded products, a segmented set counting once.

| ID | Product | Shown as | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 413 | Generic text: METAR/SPECI, TAF (incl. AMD/COR), PIREP, winds aloft | Weather tab; `/api/fisb/text` | sample: 224 / 1254 |
| 8 | NOTAM (D, FDC, TFR) | NOTAM / TFR layer | sample: 62 / 408 (points, a circular prism, an AGL polygon) |
| 11 | AIRMET | AIRMET / G-AIRMET layer | sample: 2 / 232 |
| 12 | SIGMET, convective SIGMET | SIGMET / convective layer | sample: 2 / 20 |
| 13 | SUA status (text only, so nothing to draw) | `/api/fisb/text?type=twgo` | sample: 71 / 35 |
| 14 | G-AIRMET (graphics only) | AIRMET / G-AIRMET layer | sample: 0 / 256 |
| 15 | CWA | CWA layer | vectors (in neither sample) |
| 16, 17 | NOTAM-TRA, NOTAM-TMOA | NOTAM / TFR layer | vectors (in neither sample); see the overlay note below |
| 63 | NEXRAD regional | Radar (regional) layer | sample: 200 (all empty blocks) / 898 |
| 64 | NEXRAD CONUS | Radar (CONUS) layer | sample: 0 / 1087 |
| 70, 71 | Icing low / high (per altitude code) | Other rasters | sample: 0 / 369, 117 |
| 84 | Cloud tops | Other rasters | sample: 0 / 714 |
| 90, 91 | Turbulence low / high (per altitude code) | Other rasters | sample: 0 / 440, 220 |
| 103 | Lightning | Other rasters | sample: 0 / 100 (all empty-block lists; the run-length layout is vectors only) |

The rasters other than NEXRAD (70/71/84/90/91/103) are passed through as
raw levels with a product-specific legend; where the open decoders disagree
on what a level means, the legend says "approximate". Any other product ID
is counted (`unsupported_products`) and dropped. Current Report Lists
(frame type 14) and TIS-B/ADS-R service status frames (type 15) are
recognised and skipped.

## What is skipped

These are **[U]** in the reference: no sample contains them and the open
decoders disagree, so skycued counts and drops them rather than guess.

- **APDU time options 1 and 3** (seconds-resolution times). Counted in
  `skipped_experimental`; decoded with `fisb.experimental = true`. Both
  test data use options 0 and 2 only.
- **Raster blocks at or above 60° N** (the doubled block lattice). Counted in
  `blocks.high_lat_skipped`; decoded with `fisb.experimental = true`. Neither
  sample has data there.
- **Overlay operator 1 (TRA/TMOA, products 16/17)**: two graphic records
  meant to be combined into one shape. skycue shows each record as its own
  shape and does not combine them.
- **Prism minor axis and rotation.** A circular prism (geometry 7/8) is drawn
  as a circle of its major radius; an elliptical prism's minor radius and
  angle are dropped.
- **Cloud-top level to altitude mapping**, the icing table order and the
  other disputed raster legends: levels are passed through, labelled
  approximate.

Also dropped, and counted: uplinks that are not 432 bytes or not valid hex
(`bad_frames`, `bad_hex`), uplinks whose header says the application data
is not valid (`not_app_data`), truncated APDUs (`bad_apdu`), TWGO payloads
that do not parse (`bad_products`), shapes with a point outside ±90/±180
(`rejected_shapes`), raster blocks that do not total exactly 128 bins or
leave bytes over (`blocks_rejected`), and segment sets still incomplete
after 60 min (`segments_timed_out`; at most 512 sets are held).

## How the store keeps the picture

`skycued/src/fisb/fisb_store.hpp` has the exact rules; in plain words:

- **Mosaics.** One mosaic per raster product (icing and turbulence: per
  product and altitude code). Each holds the newest block for every block
  number, and the newest APDU time seen as its product time. A block more
  than 10 min older than the mosaic is ignored; when the mosaic's time moves
  on, blocks more than 10 min older than the new time are pruned. An
  empty-block list fills its blocks with level 0, or level 1 for CONUS (an
  empty CONUS block means "no precipitation", not "no data"). At most 4096
  blocks per mosaic; past that the oldest is evicted and counted. A mosaic is
  published (WebSocket notice, fisb-stream record) 10 s after its last
  change, or every 30 s while changes keep coming. A block is a change only
  when it is new, has a newer time or other bins than the one held: the
  ground station re-sends the whole mosaic every cycle, and an identical
  repeat (`store.blocks_unchanged`) neither republishes it nor delays a
  publish that is due.
- **Text.** One METAR and one TAF per station, replaced only by a newer
  issue. Winds aloft: one per station and forecast period (6/12/24 h),
  replaced by the next issue. PIREPs: a set keyed by station, time and a hash
  of the text; a repeat changes nothing.
- **TWGO pairing** (NOTAM, AIRMET, SIGMET, SUA, G-AIRMET, CWA). A report's
  text and graphics arrive in separate APDUs and are paired by key
  (`product-year-report-location-month`). A record is shown once it is
  complete (it has text; a G-AIRMET, which has none, once it has graphics).
  An unmatched half waits up to 720 min (or until its graphics end, if
  earlier). New text replaces the old and drops
  the old graphics.
- **Keep-alives.** A NOTAM-TFR's text is sent only every other cycle; the
  other cycles carry an empty text that only renews the record (its last
  reception, so its 70-min lifetime), never blanks it.
- **Report year.** A TWGO report whose year is not the data clock's year
  (the year before is also accepted in January, the year after in December)
  is dropped and counted in `store.wrong_year`: a report stuck on the air
  from an earlier year is not current.
- **Stale on arrival.** An AIRMET, SIGMET or CWA (products 11, 12, 15; not
  G-AIRMET, not NOTAMs) whose start is more than 12 h before or more than 6 h
  after the data clock is dropped and counted in `store.stale_start`, on
  every reception. The start is the graphics start; a report without
  graphics is judged by the `ddhhmm` issuance in its text header
  (`WST KMKC 062057 ...`), taken in the month nearest the clock: a
  day-of-month more than half a month in the past resolves into the future
  and is rejected by the 6 h limit. Known limit: a text-only report exactly
  one month old has the same `ddhhmm` as now and is accepted (reports with
  graphics are covered by their start). None of these
  products is valid longer than 6 h, so such a report is stuck on the air
  (tg30 still re-broadcasts a July convective SIGMET in October). A report
  already shown is not removed by this; it is no longer renewed and is left
  to its existing expiry.
- **Not yet in force.** A report whose graphics start time is still ahead of
  the data clock is shown with `pending: true` (drawn dashed and dimmed); it
  is published again with `pending: false` once the clock reaches the start.
- **Cancellations.** A text status 0 or an object status 13 cancels: the
  record is flagged cancelled, its text and shapes cleared, and an expired
  event with reason `cancelled` goes out. For 30 min everything else for that
  key is ignored (counted in `store.ignored_after_cancel`); then the key is
  free again.
- **Expiry** (always on the data clock, below). An item already expired when
  it arrives is not stored (`store.stale`).

  | Product | Expires |
  | --- | --- |
  | NEXRAD 63/64, lightning 103 | 75 min after product time |
  | Icing, turbulence, cloud tops (70/71/84/90/91) | 105 min after product time |
  | METAR | 120 min after its observation time |
  | TAF | end of its first validity period (else 120 min after issue) |
  | PIREP | 120 min after its report time |
  | Winds aloft | 12 h after its valid time, unless the next issue replaces it |
  | NOTAM, AIRMET, SIGMET, G-AIRMET, CWA (8/11/12/14/15/16/17) | the earlier of 70 min after the last reception (a keep-alive or a repeat renews it) and the record's end (the later of the NOTAM text's end of validity and the graphics end time) |
  | SUA (13) | its end (the graphics end time); with none, 70 min after the last reception |
  | TWGO half without its partner | 720 min after its last reception (or its graphics end, if earlier) |
  | Cancelled TWGO | 30 min after the cancel |

## Time and replay

APDUs carry only partial times (hh:mm, sometimes month/day). Each uplink is
decoded against a reference time, and a partial time resolves to the
candidate nearest it. In skycued that is the uplink's own receive time;
the store then runs on the **data clock**: the newest observation or uplink
time seen, never the wall clock while replaying. So a replay of the 2015
sample shows 2015 products with 2015 ages, and they expire on their 2015
lifetimes as the replay's clock passes them; `/api/fisb/status` reports
`now` (the data clock) and `radar_age_s` against it. `skycued --replay`
feeds a recording's `uat_uplink` records to the decoder with their recorded
times, so a replay reproduces what the live run showed.

## Recordings

With `fisb.enable = true` and `jsonl` in `recording.formats`, skycued writes a
third stream, `fisb-YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS.jsonl`, next to `received-*` and
`sent-*`, rotated with them. Each record is `"time"` (the data clock) and
`"type"`:

| `type` | written when |
| --- | --- |
| `fisb_text` | a product-413 report is new or replaced |
| `fisb_twgo` | a TWGO record becomes complete or changes (shapes included) |
| `fisb_radar` | a NEXRAD mosaic is published (every block it holds) |
| `fisb_raster` | another raster layer is published |
| `fisb_expired` | an item left the store (`reason` `expired` or `cancelled`) |

The raw uplinks themselves stay in the received stream as `uat_uplink`
records whatever `fisb.enable` says, so a recording can always be decoded
later. Field-by-field: [recording-format.md](recording-format.md#fis-b-stream).

## Web API and WebSocket

All `GET`, under `/api/fisb/`: `status` (counts, mosaic heads, stations
heard; also the `fisb` key of `/api/state`), `text?type=&station=`
(METAR/TAF/PIREP/winds, or `type=twgo`), `shapes` (a GeoJSON
FeatureCollection of the TWGO shapes), `radar?product=63|64` and
`raster?product=&altitude=` (a mosaic's blocks, run-length coded, with the
palette). The WebSocket carries `fisb_text`, `fisb_shape`, `fisb_radar` /
`fisb_raster` (notifications only: fetch the blocks) and `fisb_expired`
items. The contract, with examples from the 2015 sample:
[web-api.md, FIS-B](web-api.md#fis-b).

## On the map

The layer control (top right) gains:

- **Radar (regional)** and **Radar (CONUS)**, on by default: NEXRAD in the
  standard 7-level palette, drawn at half opacity (and marked stale) once
  older than 15 min (regional) or 30 min (CONUS); it leaves at 75 min.
  A strip on the map shows the radar's age and a legend.
- **Other rasters**, off by default, with a selector for the layer (icing
  and turbulence per altitude) and its legend.
- **NOTAM / TFR** (products 8, 16, 17), **SIGMET / convective**,
  **AIRMET / G-AIRMET**, **CWA** and **SUA**, off by default. A cancelled
  shape is dashed; one not yet in force (its start still ahead, `pending`)
  is dotted and dimmed, as the note under the radar legend says. Clicking a
  shape opens a card with its report text, altitudes and validity in the
  Weather tab of the side panel.

The **Weather** button (header) opens the Weather tab of the side panel:
METAR, TAF, PIREP, winds aloft and the TWGO reports without graphics
("NOTAM / SIGMET text": text-only NOTAMs, SUA, AIRMET and SIGMET text whose
shape has not come, or was dropped by an amended text) grouped by kind,
filtered by a station
prefix (e.g. `KSF`), each with its age. The page asks for the products once
after connecting and then applies the WebSocket items.

## skycue-fisb

`skycue-fisb` runs the same decoder and store offline, for checking a
capture or a recording:

```sh
skycue-fisb --ref-date 2015-01-24 uat-sample.txt.gz   # summary (the default)
skycue-fisb --json received-20261101-120000.jsonl > products.jsonl
skycue-fisb --experimental capture.txt               # include the [U] layouts
```

Input, plain or `.gz`, line by line in any mix: dump978-fa lines
(`+hex;...`, with or without `t=`; downlinks and `#` comments are skipped),
`skycue-rawrec` captures (`<epoch_ms> +hex;`), and received-stream JSONL
(`uat_uplink` records). Several files share one decoder and store.
Uplinks without a timestamp (such as the 2015 sample) are dated by
`--ref-date YYYY-MM-DD` (default today, with a warning): they are decoded
against one estimated reception time on that date, the newest radar product
time found, else the median product-413 time, else 12:00 UTC.

`--json` writes every store event as one JSON object per line, with the
recording stream's field names; two runs on the same input are identical.
`--summary` prints inputs, products by ID, text and TWGO records, global
blocks, mosaics, ground stations, store events and every counter. Exit
status: 0, 1 an unreadable or truncated file, 2 a usage error, 3 no uplink in
the input. On the 2015 sample (abridged):


for any recording with `uat_uplink` records
([operating.md](operating.md#analysing-recordings-skycue-stats)).

## For developers

- Code: `skycued/src/fisb/` — `uplink_frame` (432-byte frame, header,
  information frames), `apdu` (APDU header, times), `dlac` (6-bit text),
  `segment_assembler`, `twgo` and `text_products`, `global_block` (raster
  blocks), `fisb_decoder` (the pipeline), `fisb_store` (the rules above),
  `fisb_json` (the shared JSON writers). `web_fisb.cpp` serves the routes;
  `www/app.js` draws the layers.
- Tests: `test_fisb_frame`, `test_fisb_twgo`, `test_fisb_blocks`,
  `test_fisb_decoder`, `test_fisb_store`, `test_fisb_web`, `fisb_cli`

  cross-checked with fisb-decode), `stats_cli`, and the Playwright cases in
  `tests/ui/` (replaying `received-with-uplinks.jsonl` and
  `received-tg30.jsonl`).
- A layout marked **[U]** in the reference stays behind
  `fisb.experimental` until a capture confirms it; add the capture as a
  sample, then move the tag to **[V]**.
